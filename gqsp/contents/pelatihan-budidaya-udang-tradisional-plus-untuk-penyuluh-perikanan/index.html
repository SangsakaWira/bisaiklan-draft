Training on Traditional-Plus Shrimp farming for Fisheries Extension Officers

Jakarta, September 2025 | In recent years, Indonesia’s shrimp sector has faced a significant downturn. Disease outbreaks, contamination, and unstable prices have collectively hindered productivity, threatening both the industry and the livelihoods of thousands of farmers who depend on it. To address these challenges, the United Nations Industrial Development Organization (UNIDO), through the Global Quality and Standards Programme (GQSP) Indonesia Phase 2, has partnered with the Indonesian Shrimp Forum (FUI) and Blueyou to roll out initiatives aimed at improving both productivity and quality through sustainable practices. 

Two flagship projects have emerged from this collaboration,namely Traditional Plus Shrimp Farming (TPSF) and Selva Shrimp, also known as integrated mangrove–shrimp farming. Pilot projects in several regions have shown encouraging results, with farmers reporting higher yields and premium-quality shrimp. 

Recognizing the importance of scaling up these benefits, UNIDO has taken steps to expand the program nationwide. A key strategy involves equipping fisheries extension officers, who serve as the crucial link between government policies and local farmers and play a vital role in disseminating technology, providing technical guidance, and connecting farmers to national programs and new market opportunities. 

As part of this effort, a two-day training session was held on 16 and 17 September 2025, bringing together over 100 fisheries extension officers from across Indonesia. The training went beyond theory, offering participants first-hand insights from experts and practitioners who shared both the advantages and challenges of implementing TPSF and Selva Shrimp practices. The journey does not end here. Extension officers will soon be invited to visit project sites during the next shrimp farming cycle, allowing them to observe and engage directly in the field. By doing so, they will be better equipped to guide farmers in adopting such sustainable practices. 

With support from international partners and national networks, the programme aims to restore Indonesia’s position as a leading shrimp producer while ensuring environmental and economic sustainability. 

GQSP Indonesia Phase 2 strengthens quality and standards in Indonesia’s fisheries value chains.
