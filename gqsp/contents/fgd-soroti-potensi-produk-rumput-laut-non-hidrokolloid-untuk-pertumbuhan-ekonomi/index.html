FGD Highlights Untapped Potential of Non-Hydrocolloid Seaweed Products for Indonesia’s Economic Growth

Jakarta, 23 April 2025 — The Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries (MMAF), through the Tropical Seaweed Innovation Network (TSIN) under the Directorate of Product Competitiveness (DJPDS), in collaboration with UNIDO’s Global Quality and Standards Programme (GQSP) Indonesia, hosted the first in a series of Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) to support the development of the Seaweed Roadmap under the National Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMN) 2025–2029.

Held at the Arwana Room, Mina Bahari II Building, the hybrid event gathered over 500 (45 offline and 485 online) stakeholders from government bodies, industry associations, academia, and the private sector. The FGD focused on the theme: “Uncovering Business Opportunities for Non-Hydrocolloid Seaweed Products.”

Key resource speakers included Mr. Erwin Dwiyana (Director of Marketing, DJPDS-PKP), Prof. Dr. Ir. Nurjanah from IPB University, and representatives from the Ministry of Agriculture and the Ministry of Industry. Discussions centered around five strategic areas: raw material availability, product development, market potential, technological readiness, and policy support.

Stakeholders highlighted the urgent need to map seaweed production areas to stabilize supply and pricing. Mr. Pontas from ASTRULI emphasized the lack of a national database and suggested focusing on species such as Spinosum, Gracilaria, Ulva, and Sargassum to diversify raw material sources. Ms. Lin (UNDANA) supported the idea, noting that proper mapping is essential for strategy planning. In terms of product innovation, Mr. Boedi S. Julianto (PT. JASUDA) proposed developing biostimulants, aquafeed, bioplastics, and cosmetics. Ms. Lin recommended a phased product rollout over five years, beginning with food products and gradually expanding to fertilizers and biofuels. Regulatory readiness, especially for cosmetics and health products, was noted as a key challenge. The discussion also revealed growing international demand, particularly for Ulva as a food-grade export, with Norway identified as a promising market. Enhancing compliance with food-grade standards and building MSME capacity to support export chains were emphasized.

On technology, Mr. Boedi called for stronger collaboration between academia and industry, while Mr. Sudari (UNIDO-GQSP) highlighted the need for foreign investment to accelerate technology development.Government policy support remains critical. Suggestions included facilitating biostimulant application in agriculture and aquaculture, supporting MSME certification processes, and updating regulatory frameworks such as KBLI codes and fiscal incentives. Additional inputs included proposals to create a seaweed-focused academic program (UNDIP), boost local nori production (PT. Nori Noribet), and support mini-industries at the village level (Mina Agar Makmur Cooperative).

This FGD marks a key milestone in advancing Indonesia’s seaweed industry beyond hydrocolloids. The insights gathered will feed into future discussions and inform strategic policymaking for inclusive and sustainable seaweed sector growth under the 2025–2029 RPJMN.
